What General Liability Looks Like for Fort Myers Businesses

Fort Myers is the commercial hub of Southwest Florida, and that changes the character of general liability exposure here compared with the smaller cities around it. The city’s permitting runs through its own community development department inside city limits, while work in the surrounding unincorporated areas falls to Lee County — and contractors who cross that boundary regularly end up carrying certificates that have to satisfy two different sets of project requirements.

The commercial corridors along Cleveland Avenue, Colonial Boulevard, and Daniels Parkway carry a mix of retail, medical office, and light industrial tenants. Landlords in these centers almost always require a certificate of insurance naming them as an additional insured before a tenant takes occupancy, and many require it renewed annually. For contractors, the general contractors managing these buildouts push the same requirement downstream — no certificate, no site access.

Downtown’s River District is its own risk profile. A dense cluster of restaurants, bars, and event venues means high pedestrian volume on sidewalks the businesses don’t own, outdoor seating on public right-of-way, and the customer-injury claims that come with both. Businesses serving alcohol should be looking at whether their general liability policy addresses liquor liability or excludes it, because the two are frequently separated.

The rebuild activity following Hurricane Ian also left a lasting mark on how work gets papered in this market. Property owners and their carriers became far more rigorous about verifying that every trade on a job carried its own coverage, rather than relying on the general contractor’s policy to cover everyone. If you are a subcontractor here and your certificate lapses, you will hear about it before you finish the week.

General contractors in the Fort Myers market typically require each subcontractor to carry its own general liability policy and to furnish a certificate naming the GC as an additional insured — relying on the GC’s policy alone is rarely accepted and can leave a sub without a defense if a claim names them directly.

Standard general liability covers customer slip-and-fall and property damage claims, but it commonly excludes liquor liability. A downtown Fort Myers restaurant or bar serving alcohol usually needs that exposure addressed separately, and many landlords in the district specifically require it in the lease.

The coverage itself does not change, but the entities you are asked to name as additional insureds do. A job inside Fort Myers city limits and one in unincorporated Lee County can involve different owners, GCs, and project requirements — which is why contractors here often need certificates issued per project rather than one blanket document.

Florida requires certified and registered contractors to demonstrate general liability coverage as part of licensure through the state, with minimum limits that vary by license classification. That state requirement is separate from — and usually lower than — what a Fort Myers general contractor or commercial landlord will contractually demand of you.
