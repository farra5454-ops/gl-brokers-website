Charlotte County Rules and Waterfront Risk in Punta Gorda

The first thing contractors moving into Punta Gorda discover is that their Lee County habits do not transfer cleanly. Punta Gorda is in Charlotte County, and permitting runs through the city for work inside city limits and through Charlotte County’s building division for the surrounding unincorporated areas. The insurance requirements themselves are governed by state law and by contract rather than by county, but the owners, general contractors, and project documents are different — which means new certificates, new additional insureds, and new project-specific paperwork.

The waterfront drives much of the local liability picture. Punta Gorda Isles is built around a canal system, and Charlotte Harbor and the Peace River define the city’s edge. Dock, seawall, and boat lift contractors working here face the same coverage trap their Cape Coral counterparts do: general liability policies frequently exclude work performed from watercraft, and crews operating off barges or floating platforms can fall under federal Longshore and Harbor Workers’ coverage instead of Florida’s workers’ compensation system. Both points need confirming in writing, not assuming.

Punta Gorda’s building stock adds a distinctive renovation exposure. The city rebuilt substantially after Hurricane Charley in 2004 and again after Ian in 2022, but its historic downtown retains older structures with shared walls, aging systems, and construction methods that predate current practice. Renovation work in that environment routinely uncovers conditions outside the original scope, and work that affects an adjoining building creates third-party property damage exposure with a neighbor who never signed your contract.

On the commercial side, Fishermen’s Village and the surrounding waterfront district concentrate visitor traffic onto docks, boardwalks, and elevated walkways. That is a different premises profile than an inland storefront — wet surfaces, elevation changes, and water access all raise the severity of a fall. Operators in these spaces typically face landlord certificate requirements alongside genuinely elevated exposure, and setting limits against peak-season visitor volume rather than an annual average is the sounder approach.
