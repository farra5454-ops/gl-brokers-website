Estero's Building Boom and What It Means for Liability

Estero incorporated as a village on December 31, 2014, and construction has not slowed since. Gulf Shore Business reported an estimated $422.1 million in residential and commercial building permits in 2025, the second-highest year since incorporation, behind 2024's $598.3 million. Projects named in that report include RiverCreek, Lumio Estero, Woodfield, Downtown Estero, and Coconut Pointe Residences.

For contractors, all that new work creates liability that outlasts the job. Under Florida Statutes § 95.11(3)(b), a construction claim can generally be brought up to 7 years after the certificate of occupancy or completion. A leak, a failed slab, or a cracked driveway that shows up years later is a completed operations claim, so the completed operations limit on your policy deserves as much attention as the per-occurrence limit.

For retailers and restaurants, the exposure is foot traffic. Coconut Point and Miromar Outlets draw shoppers from across Lee and Collier counties, and Hertz Arena brings event crowds. Customer injuries on property you control, such as slips, trips, and falls, are the core general liability risk for those businesses.

Who We Insure in Estero

General liability needs in Estero depend on where you work and who you sign contracts with.

Shopping Center Tenants

Stores and service businesses at Coconut Point and Miromar Outlets sign leases that set the general liability limits and usually require the property owner and management company to be named as additional insureds. Center management typically checks the certificate before you open and at each renewal.

Restaurants & Hospitality

Restaurants along Corkscrew Road, Ben Hill Griffin Parkway, and US-41 handle high customer turnover, outdoor seating, and wet floors. Each of those adds premises exposure, and most restaurant leases require coverage before opening.

Residential & Build-Out Contractors

Crews building Estero's new communities, and tenant improvement contractors working inside occupied centers, need certificates for every GC and property owner they work for. Build-outs often run overnight with tenants and shoppers a few feet away.

Event Vendors & Pop-Up Sellers

Vendors working community events, shopping center promotions, and arena events usually need a certificate before setup, often naming the organizer or venue as an additional insured for the event dates.

Retail Leases and Certificates of Insurance

For an Estero retailer or restaurant, the lease usually decides your general liability, not state law. Shopping center leases typically spell out the required limits, name the property owner and its management company as additional insureds, and require notice before the policy changes or cancels. If your certificate doesn't match the lease, center management can hold up your opening or your renewal.

The fastest path is to send us the insurance section of your lease before you buy. We match the policy and the certificate wording to it, so the first certificate you hand over is the one the landlord accepts.

Build-Outs Inside Operating Shopping Centers

Tenant improvement work at Estero's centers happens inside buildings that stay open for business. Shoppers, employees, and neighboring tenants are often a few feet from active work, and much of it runs overnight. That is why GCs and property owners commonly require every trade to carry its own general liability, to name both of them as additional insureds, and to include primary and non-contributory wording and a waiver of subrogation.

Permits for that work come from the Village of Estero's Building and Permitting Services when the job is inside village limits. Gulf Coast Town Center and Florida Gulf Coast University are nearby but sit north of the village, so work there goes through Lee County instead. Florida contractors also carry a licensing requirement: under § 489.115, certified and registered contractors must attest to carrying public liability and property damage insurance, with minimums set by Rule 61G4-15.003.
