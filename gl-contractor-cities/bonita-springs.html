The Work Driving Bonita Springs Construction and the Risk It Carries

Underwriters price a Bonita Springs contractor by the work, not the zip code. These are the jobs we see most, and the liability question each one raises.

Condo & High-Rise Restoration

Florida’s post-Surfside milestone inspection requirements (§ 553.899) and condo reserve rules have increased demand for concrete restoration, balcony, railing, and waterproofing work on coastal towers, including those along Bonita Beach and Bonita Bay. That work happens over occupied units and shared parking, and some contractor policies restrict condo, multi-unit, or high-rise work, so it has to be disclosed and written for.

Flood Repair, Elevation & Storm Rebuilds

Hurricane Irma caused serious flooding along the Imperial River in 2017, and Hurricane Ian’s storm surge reached Bonita Beach and Little Hickory Island in 2022. Repair and elevation work on older homes can run into FEMA’s 50% substantial-improvement rule mid-project, and scope changes like that are a common source of owner disputes.

Custom Homes in Gated Communities

High-value builds and remodels in Bonita Bay, Pelican Landing, Spanish Wells, and similar communities usually mean an association vendor file: a certificate on record before the gate lets your crew in, often naming the association as certificate holder or additional insured, plus damage to common areas, irrigation, and landscaping as a real exposure.

Commercial Build-Outs on US-41 & Bonita Beach Road

Tenant improvements in the plazas along the Tamiami Trail and Bonita Beach Road put trades inside operating centers with customers nearby. Commercial GCs and landlords typically require $1M per-occurrence limits, additional insured status, primary and non-contributory wording, and a waiver of subrogation.

Bonita Springs Permitting & Compliance Realities

Know whose permit counter you are standing at. Bonita Springs incorporated in 1999 and issues its own building permits inside city limits. A job a mile outside those limits goes through Lee County instead, and anything south of the county line in North Naples falls under Collier County or the City of Naples. Contractors who work all three regularly keep their insurance paperwork current with each office separately.

No workers’ comp proof, no permit. Florida Statutes § 440.103 makes proof of workers’ compensation coverage, or a valid exemption, a condition of receiving a building permit anywhere in Florida. Under § 440.02, construction businesses need coverage from their first employee, compared with four employees for most other industries.

Your license depends on general liability. To be certified or registered, a Florida contractor must attest to carrying public liability and property damage insurance under § 489.115, with minimums set by Rule 61G4-15.003. In practice those minimums are a floor. Many GCs, developers, and condo boards ask for $1M per occurrence and $2M aggregate in their contracts.

GCs verify, and they are liable if they don’t. Under § 440.10, a contractor picks up workers’ comp responsibility for an uninsured subcontractor’s employees. That is why many GCs check the Division of Workers’ Compensation proof-of-coverage records and exemption status before your first day. Uninsured employers also face stop-work orders and penalties of twice the premium they should have paid under § 440.107.

Core Coverages for Bonita Springs Trades

General liability is the policy every Bonita Springs job asks for first. These are the policies that usually sit beside it.

General Liability

Covers third-party bodily injury and property damage from your operations and completed work: a visitor hurt on your site, a neighbor’s seawall or pool cracked by equipment, a leak that shows up in the unit below months after you finish. For Bonita Springs work, check three things: completed operations limits (Florida construction defect claims can surface years after a job), any condo or multi-unit exclusion, and water, mold, or subsidence endorsements that narrow coverage in low-lying ground.

Workers’ Compensation

Required from the first employee in Florida construction, and priced by NCCI class code and payroll. Roofing (5551), electrical (5190), plumbing (5183), HVAC (5537), and residential carpentry (5645) are among the most common we place for Bonita Springs crews. Expect an annual payroll audit: uninsured subcontractor payments get added to your payroll and billed at your rates. Larger contractors also carry an experience modification rate (EMR) that GCs check during prequalification.

Tools, Equipment & Excess Limits

General liability never covers your own property. Tools stolen from a job box or an unlocked trailer overnight, or a mixer damaged on site, fall under contractor’s equipment coverage (inland marine), which is worth asking about when we quote. When a condo board or commercial GC wants more than $1M, an excess or umbrella policy stacks additional limits over your GL, auto, and employer’s liability.

Flood-Zone Work: Where Bonita Springs GL Claims Start

Much of Bonita Springs sits low, between the Imperial River, Spring Creek, Estero Bay, and the Gulf. That geography turns ordinary site work into a liability question. When fill, grading, or a new driveway sends stormwater onto the next lot, the neighbor’s damage is third-party property damage. That is the kind of claim general liability is designed for, and a common source of neighbor disputes in low-lying areas.

Elevation and substantial-improvement work brings its own pattern. Once repair costs on an older home approach half its value, floodplain rules can force the structure up to current elevation, turning a remodel into a lift-and-rebuild partway through. Insurance doesn’t solve a permitting problem, but the change orders, delays, and re-work that follow are often where owner disputes and allegations against contractors begin. Document scope changes in writing, and make sure your policy’s completed operations coverage reaches the work you’re standing behind.

Bonita Springs Contractor Insurance FAQ

Almost always, and Florida law is the reason. Under Florida Statutes § 440.10, a contractor becomes responsible for the workers’ compensation of a subcontractor’s employees if that subcontractor has no coverage of its own. GCs protect themselves by collecting a workers’ comp certificate or a valid state exemption from every sub before work starts, and most also require a general liability certificate naming them as additional insured. There is a cost angle too: at audit, carriers typically add payments made to uninsured subs to the GC’s payroll, which raises the GC’s premium.

If your policy is already placed with us, we can often turn a certificate around the same day. Call (239) 898-2323 rather than using the web form when it is urgent. Have the certificate holder’s exact legal name and address, the project address, and any wording the GC or association requires, such as additional insured or waiver of subrogation. For a brand-new policy, the certificate is issued once coverage is bound, which is often the same day when your business details are ready.

Exemptions are issued statewide by the Florida Division of Workers’ Compensation, not by Lee County or the City of Bonita Springs, so the rules are the same anywhere in Florida. In construction, Florida Statutes § 440.02 allows no more than three officers or LLC members to be exempt, each owning at least 10% of the business, and § 440.05 requires them to be listed with the Florida Division of Corporations. An exemption is valid for two years and applies only to the named owner. It does not cover employees, and a construction business needs a workers’ comp policy once it has an employee. Some GCs and condo associations will not accept an exemption in place of a policy, so check before you bid.

Inside city limits, permits are issued by the City of Bonita Springs rather than Lee County. Florida Statutes § 440.103 requires proof of workers’ compensation coverage, or a valid exemption, before a building permit is issued, and under § 489.115, certified and registered contractors must carry general liability insurance in amounts set by Florida Administrative Code Rule 61G4-15.003. Registration paperwork changes, so confirm the current list with the city’s building division before you apply.

Some contractor policies carry exclusions or restrictions for condominium, multi-unit residential, or work above a certain building height, which is exactly the kind of work that has grown in coastal Bonita Springs since Florida’s post-Surfside milestone inspection law (Florida Statutes § 553.899). If you do balcony, concrete restoration, waterproofing, or window work on mid- and high-rise buildings, tell us up front so the policy is written for it rather than discovering an exclusion after a claim.

Sudden, accidental damage to a third party’s property caused by your operations (for example, fill or regrading that pushes water onto an adjoining lot) is generally the kind of claim general liability is built for, subject to your policy’s terms and any water or subsidence endorsements. It does not cover storm flood damage to your own client’s building, which is a flood insurance question, and it does not pay to repair your own faulty work.
