Flood-Zone Work and Local Liability in Bonita Springs

Bonita Springs has a geography problem that shapes its contractor liability. The Imperial River runs through the middle of the city, the Gulf sits to the west, and a significant portion of the developed area lies in low elevation. Flooding here is not hypothetical — the city has taken serious water in multiple storm events, and the drainage decisions made on individual jobsites accumulate into neighborhood-scale outcomes.

For site work, grading, fill, and drainage contractors, that creates an exposure that is easy to underestimate. If your fill or regrade sends water onto the adjoining lot, the neighbor’s damage is third-party property damage, and these disputes are among the more persistent claims in low-lying Lee County. The claim is rarely about the quality of your work on your own client’s property — it is about where the water went afterward, which is exactly the kind of consequence a general liability policy exists to address.

The city runs its own building and permitting function, and elevation and floodplain requirements are a live part of that review. Contractors doing substantial improvement work on older structures near the river encounter elevation certificates and floodplain rules that can change the scope mid-project. Coverage does not resolve a permitting problem, but the delays and re-work these reviews create are frequently where disputes with owners begin.

On the commercial side, the Bonita Beach Road corridor between Tamiami Trail and the beach carries a steady mix of retail, restaurants, and service businesses drawing both residents and beach traffic. Landlords along this corridor typically require tenants to carry general liability naming the landlord as an additional insured, and the seasonal swing in customer volume between winter and summer is worth accounting for when setting limits rather than averaging it out across the year.
