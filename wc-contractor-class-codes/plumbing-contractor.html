Plumbing Contractors: Workers Comp Insurance in Florida

Plumbing work means crawling through tight spaces and handling hazardous materials, and Florida's humidity and hurricane season make job sites riskier still. Workers' Compensation isn't just compliance; it's how you protect your crew and your business.

Quick Answer

Plumbing contractors in Florida need workers' comp coverage once they have just 1 employee. The primary class code is Class Code 5183, with an advisory rate of $2.737 per $100 of payroll. Skipping coverage risks a $1,000/day stop-work order and 2× unpaid premiums.

Which Class Code and Rate Apply to Plumbing Contractors in Florida?

Getting the NCCI class code right matters a great deal for Florida plumbing contractors, since it drives the workers' compensation premium calculation. The primary code, 5183 — Plumbing NOC and Drivers, covers employees doing plumbing system installation, repair, and maintenance, including water supply, drain/waste/vent, and gas line work. As of January 1, 2026, the Florida advisory rate for Class Code 5183 is $2.737 per $100 of payroll. Employees who handle clerical duties exclusively can instead be classified under Class Code 8810 — Clerical Office Employees, which carries a much lower rate and can meaningfully reduce premium when payroll is properly segregated. The premium itself comes from multiplying payroll (per $100) by the classification rate, then adjusting for the experience modification factor (EMR). Because this is a payroll-based system, higher payroll sitting in a higher-rated code drives the premium up, which is why accurate classification and clean payroll segregation matter so much for a fair assessment.

Do Plumbing Contractors in Florida Need Workers' Comp?

Florida Statute 440 is the governing law here, and it requires construction industry employers, plumbing contractors included, to carry workers' compensation coverage once they have even one employee, whether full-time or part-time. The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR), which handles plumbing contractor licensing, backs this up by requiring proof of workers' compensation insurance or a valid exemption within 30 days of license issuance. Skipping coverage brings serious consequences, including stop-work orders, substantial fines, and in some cases criminal charges. Contractors also carry responsibility for their subcontractors: if a sub doesn't carry their own workers' compensation insurance, the primary contractor can be held liable for that sub's injuries, and the sub's payroll may get folded into the contractor's policy during an audit. That's exactly why verifying subcontractor coverage with certificates of insurance is worth the effort.

Common Coverage Mistakes Florida Plumbing Contractors Make

A handful of mistakes show up again and again among Florida plumbing contractors managing workers' compensation. Misclassifying employees as independent contractors is a common one: if someone is functionally an employee, misclassifying them can trigger serious penalties, including back premiums and legal liability, especially in construction. Owner exclusions trip people up too. Corporate officers or LLC members with at least 10% ownership can seek an exemption, but filing it incorrectly or misunderstanding what it covers can leave real gaps in coverage. Poor tracking of subcontractor certificates of insurance is another frequent problem, since an uninsured subcontractor's payroll can get added to the primary contractor's policy during an audit, driving premiums up. And some contractors overlook the gap between workers' comp and general liability policies. Workers' comp covers employee injuries, while general liability handles third-party bodily injury and property damage, and knowing where one ends and the other begins is essential to avoid being uninsured where it counts.

That covers licensed plumbing contractors, apprentices, and helpers alike. Because plumbing is classified as a construction trade under Florida law, there's no employee-count exemption to fall back on — even a single employee triggers the requirement.

Most plumbing work in Florida falls under NCCI class code 5183 (Plumbing — Not Otherwise Classified), which covers installation, repair, and service of plumbing systems in residential and commercial buildings. Gas fitting work sometimes stays under 5183 and sometimes gets rated under a related code, depending on the carrier. Office staff are rated separately, under 8810. Getting the class code right matters a lot here — misclassification is one of the leading causes of unexpected audit bills for plumbing contractors.

At the close of each policy year, the insurance carrier audits actual payroll to reconcile what was paid against what was actually owed. If payroll grew over the year, whether from hiring more plumbers, logging more overtime, or bringing on helpers, an audit bill follows for the difference. Plumbing contractors can head off surprise audit bills by reporting payroll changes to their agent as they happen, switching to pay-as-you-go billing, or looking into PEO (employee leasing) arrangements that remove the annual audit altogether.

Corporate officers at a plumbing company can apply for an exemption from workers' compensation coverage under Florida Statute 440.02, but that exemption covers only the officer personally — every other employee still needs coverage. Exemptions have to be filed with the Florida Division of Workers' Compensation and renewed every two years. Let one expire, or file it incorrectly, and the officer is left personally exposed to liability for work-related injuries.

Operating without required workers' comp coverage triggers an immediate stop-work order from the Florida Division of Workers' Compensation, which shuts down every job site the contractor has running. Fines get calculated at twice the premium that should have been paid, with a $1,000 minimum. Beyond that, the contractor becomes personally liable for all medical costs, lost wages, and disability benefits owed to any injured employee — exposure that can climb into six figures fast for a serious plumbing injury like a fall or a pipe explosion.
