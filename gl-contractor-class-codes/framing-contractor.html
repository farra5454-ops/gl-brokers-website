Framing Contractors: General Liability Insurance in Florida

Florida's residential and commercial growth creates real opportunity for framing contractors, and real risk, from hurricane season to strict building codes. General Liability is what stands between a framing business and the incidents that come with the work.

Quick Answer

The Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) requires Florida framing contractors to carry General Liability coverage. The real-world standard runs $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate — nearly every GC, property manager, and commercial client will ask for that limit. The DBPR licensing floor of $300K won't be enough to actually land work. Completed operations coverage is required under Florida Statute 627.441.

How Is General Liability Classified for Florida Framing Contractors?

Getting the General Liability (GL) class code right matters a great deal for accurate premium calculation on a Florida framing contractor's policy. NCCI codes exist primarily for Workers' Compensation, but GL policies run on a similar classification logic to categorize the specific risks tied to framing operations. For carpentry and framing work, the two codes that matter most are 91340, for 'Carpentry — Construction of Residential Property Not Exceeding Three Stories in Height,' and 91342, for general 'Carpentry' work, both reflecting the structural risks inherent to the trade. In Florida, GL premium gets built by multiplying a contractor's annual revenue by a specific trade rate, then applying a limit multiplier. Framing carries a higher risk profile than most trades, so the trade rate per $1,000 of revenue tends to sit on the higher end, typically $10.00 to $14.50. The limit multiplier shifts based on the coverage limits chosen, with higher limits like $2M/$4M or $5M/$10M pushing the premium up. Most policies land around a $750 minimum premium.

What Are Florida's GL Requirements for Framing Contractors?

Florida framing contractors have specific legal requirements to meet around General Liability insurance. Florida Statute Chapter 627, and Section 627.441 in particular, addresses commercial general liability policies and completed operations coverage for contractors. On top of that, the Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB), under the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR), requires general and building contractors to maintain specific liability insurance. The minimum typically runs $300,000 for bodily injury and $50,000 for property damage, though many projects and clients ask for higher limits than that. Coverage isn't optional for licensed contractors operating legally in the state, and falling short, especially working without a proper license or adequate insurance, carries severe penalties under Florida Statute § 489.127, ranging from a first-degree misdemeanor on a first offense up to significant fines and potential imprisonment. Contractors working on public projects should also know they may fall under owner-controlled insurance programs, which require insurers to offer extended completed operations coverage.

Common GL Coverage Mistakes Florida Framing Contractors Make

A handful of coverage mistakes show up again and again among Florida framing contractors managing their General Liability policies. Failing to secure proper Certificates of Insurance (COIs) from subcontractors is one of the biggest, since without valid COIs, the general contractor can be held directly liable for anything a subcontractor causes, which adds up to substantial out-of-pocket costs. Confusing the roles of General Liability and Workers' Compensation is another common error: GL covers third-party bodily injury and property damage, while Workers' Comp handles employee injuries, and assuming one covers the other opens a dangerous, costly gap. Misclassifying employees as independent contractors to sidestep Workers' Comp can backfire too, since those same individuals may still count as employees in a GL claim, leaving the contractor exposed either way. And overlooking construction defect coverage, particularly around faulty workmanship or materials, can mean going uninsured for structural issues that surface well after a project wraps.

The Most Common Risks Florida Framing Contractors Face

Accidental damage to a client's existing property during construction, a dropped beam damaging a foundation being a classic example, is one of the most common claim scenarios for Florida framing contractors. Bodily injury claims come up too, when a visitor or subcontractor gets hurt on the job site because of falling debris or unsafe conditions.

What Does General Liability Actually Cover for Framing Contractors?

At its core, General Liability for framing contractors pays for third-party bodily injury and property damage claims, medical expenses for injured parties, repair or replacement costs for damaged property, and legal defense fees if the business gets sued, regardless of who's ultimately at fault.

What Determines GL Insurance Costs for Florida Framing Contractors?

A handful of factors combine to set what Florida framing contractors actually pay for General Liability. Annual revenue is a primary driver, since premiums are often calculated as a rate per $1,000 of revenue, so higher revenue generally means higher exposure and a bigger premium. Claims history plays a critical role too, a track record of frequent or severe GL claims pushes rates up because of the perceived higher risk. The experience modification rate (e-mod) is primarily a Workers' Compensation concept, but a poor e-mod can still signal broader risk management issues that indirectly affect GL pricing. How much a contractor relies on subcontractors matters as well, since inadequately insured subs transfer their exposure back to the general contractor, raising premiums. Florida's hurricane exposure is its own major factor, with the elevated risk of property damage claims in storm-prone areas pushing GL rates up across construction trades, framing included. And the risks inherent to framing work itself, falls, structural collapses, and property damage among them, get factored directly into the base premium.
