Exemptions, Volume, and Where Lehigh Acres Crews Go Wrong

Lehigh Acres is unincorporated, so there is no city government and permitting runs entirely through Lee County. The community was platted in the 1950s into a vast grid of small residential lots, and that inventory has sustained years of high-volume single-family construction. The result is an economy dominated by trade subcontractors — framing, concrete, roofing, stucco, electrical — working at speed and at thin margins. Nearly every workers’ compensation question here falls under Florida’s construction rule, which requires coverage from the very first employee.

Exemptions are where this market gets into trouble most often. Florida permits corporate officers and LLC members in the construction industry to elect an exemption, but the conditions are specific: at least ten percent ownership, and no more than three exempt officers per corporation. Sole proprietors and partners in construction are treated as employees and cannot exempt out at all. Small crews frequently attempt to structure around the requirement by putting everyone on paper as an officer, and that arrangement collapses under the three-exemption cap — leaving workers uncovered and the business exposed.

The second recurring problem is statutory contractor liability. Florida makes a contractor responsible for ensuring that its subcontractors carry workers’ compensation, and when a sub does not, the sub’s employees are generally treated as the contractor’s own for coverage purposes. In a market where work moves through layers of small crews, a contractor who accepts a handshake instead of a certificate can end up owning an injury claim for someone they never hired — along with the effect on their own premium and claim history.

The class codes that dominate Lehigh Acres are also among the most expensive in Florida. Framing, roofing, and concrete work carry high rates because the injuries are frequent and severe — falls from height, crush injuries, and heat exposure through Southwest Florida summers. That makes payroll accuracy and correct class allocation financially significant rather than clerical. Under-reporting payroll produces a substantial adjustment when the carrier audits, and in a thin-margin, high-volume market that adjustment can arrive at a genuinely difficult moment.
