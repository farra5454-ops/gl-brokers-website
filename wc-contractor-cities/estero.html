A Young Village With Two Kinds of Employers

Estero incorporated as a village on December 31, 2014, and it has been building ever since. According to Gulf Shore Business, the village issued an estimated $422.1 million in residential and commercial building permits in 2025, across 5,366 permits, following $598.3 million in 2024. Village staff reported nearly 20 active projects heading into 2026, representing about 2,500 residential units and 300,000 square feet of commercial space.

That puts Estero employers on both sides of Florida's workers' comp rules at once. On one side are the contractors: framing, concrete, roofing, electrical, and finish crews working new communities along the Corkscrew Road corridor, residential projects near the Estero River, and tenant build-outs. They need coverage from their first employee. On the other side are the retailers, restaurants, and event staff serving Coconut Point, Miromar Outlets, and Hertz Arena, where the four-employee rule applies.

One local detail worth getting right: Gulf Coast Town Center and Florida Gulf Coast University are close by, but both sit north of the village limits. Jobs there go through Lee County's permitting process rather than the village's.

Who Needs Workers' Comp in Estero?

The answer depends on the work your employees do, not on the size of your storefront.

New-Home & Build-Out Contractors

Tenant improvement work inside Coconut Point and Miromar, and framing or concrete work on new residential projects east of I-75, is construction. Under § 440.02, that means coverage from the first employee, with corporate officers and LLC members counted unless they hold an exemption.

Retail Stores & Mall Tenants

Shops at Coconut Point and Miromar Outlets reach the four-employee threshold faster than many owners expect, because the Florida Division of Workers' Compensation counts full-time and part-time employees alike. Two full-time and two part-time workers already makes four.

Restaurants & Event Staff

Commercial kitchens bring burns, cuts, slips on wet floors, and lifting injuries, and restaurant staffs often pass four employees early. Event and concession staffing around Hertz Arena can push headcount up quickly during the hockey and concert season.

Out-of-State Crews

Contractors coming from other states to work Estero's growth need to check their policy. Under § 440.10(1)(g), employers with employees working in Florida need a Florida policy or endorsement that uses Florida class codes and rates.

Pulling a Permit in the Village of Estero

Inside village limits, building permits come from the Village of Estero's Building and Permitting Services, part of its Community Development department. Work just north of the village, in areas such as San Carlos Park, Three Oaks, and around Gulf Coast Town Center, goes through Lee County. Head south into Bonita Springs and you are dealing with that city's building division instead.

Whichever office you use, the workers' comp rule is the same. Under Florida Statutes § 440.103, an employer must show proof of workers' compensation coverage, or a valid exemption certificate, every time it applies for a building permit. A lapsed certificate means the permit waits.

Retail and Restaurant Staffing: Watching the Four-Employee Line

For Estero's shops and restaurants, the coverage question usually comes down to counting correctly. Part-time workers count, so a store with two full-time and three part-time employees is at five, not two. Seasonal hiring matters too. Many Estero retailers and restaurants staff up for the winter season and the holidays, and a business that hires its fourth employee in November needs coverage from that point, not at its next renewal.

The practical habit is to check headcount before each busy season. If you are close to four, it is usually easier to put a policy in place ahead of the hiring rush than to discover the gap after someone gets hurt.

Build-Out Contractors and the Subcontractor Chain

Tenant improvement work inside occupied centers often runs overnight, through several layers of subcontractors. Under Florida Statutes § 440.10, a contractor is generally liable for the workers' comp of a subcontractor's employees when that sub has no coverage of its own. If an uninsured sub's worker is hurt on your Estero job, the claim can come back to you.

There is a cost at audit, too. Carriers typically add payments made to uninsured subs to your payroll, which raises your premium after the policy year ends. That is why many GCs managing work at Estero's shopping centers collect and verify a certificate or exemption from every sub before granting site access, and check it against the Florida Division of Workers' Compensation proof-of-coverage records.
