Two Thresholds, One Business: Bonita Springs Employers

Bonita Springs sits between the Gulf beaches and the Imperial River, and its economy reflects that split. Beach-season hospitality and retail drive one part of the local employment base while residential construction and property services drive another. For workers’ compensation, this matters because Florida applies two different thresholds, and a fair number of Bonita Springs businesses operate on both sides of the line simultaneously.

Non-construction employers generally need coverage once they reach four employees, counting part-time and seasonal staff. Construction employers need it from the first employee, with no small-crew allowance. A property management company with office staff and a maintenance crew, or a restaurant group doing its own buildout work, can be applying both rules at once — and the construction rule governs the construction work regardless of how few people perform it.

The seasonal swing along the Bonita Beach Road corridor makes the non-construction threshold a moving target. Restaurants and shops that run lean through the summer commonly staff up substantially for the winter season, crossing from three employees to six or seven and acquiring a coverage obligation partway through the year. Because the requirement attaches when the threshold is met rather than when the business reviews it, the safest practice is checking headcount at the start of each season.

The claim profile for outdoor work here is worth naming directly. Bonita Springs supports year-round landscaping and grounds maintenance, which means crews working through Southwest Florida summers where heat index routinely exceeds one hundred degrees. Heat-related illness is a legitimate workers’ compensation exposure alongside the more obvious equipment injuries, and it is one that employers tend to plan for less. Employers doing repair and elevation work on older structures near the Imperial River should also expect that scope changes triggered by floodplain review can extend crew hours and shift the work performed, which affects class code allocation and payroll reporting.
