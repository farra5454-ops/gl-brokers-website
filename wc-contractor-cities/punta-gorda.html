Marine Crews and Charlotte County Compliance

Punta Gorda is the only city in our service area located in Charlotte County rather than Lee or Collier, and contractors expanding north sometimes assume that changes their insurance obligations. It does not — Florida’s workers’ compensation thresholds are set at the state level and apply identically across county lines. What does change is the permitting authority, which runs through the City of Punta Gorda inside city limits and Charlotte County’s building division in the surrounding unincorporated areas.

The genuinely distinctive local issue is the water. Punta Gorda Isles is built around a canal system, and Charlotte Harbor and the Peace River define the city’s edge. Crews building docks and seawalls or servicing vessels work over navigable water, often from barges or floating platforms, and that location can move an injury out of Florida’s state system and into the federal Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act. A Florida state policy is not built to respond to a Longshore claim, which leaves marine employers carrying only state coverage with a real gap.

Whether Longshore applies turns on the specific nature and location of the work rather than on the employer’s job title for the crew, so it is worth reviewing operations directly against that question. Marine contractors here often need Longshore coverage written or endorsed alongside their Florida policy, and their general liability checked separately for watercraft exclusions that would undercut coverage on the same work.

Renovation work in Punta Gorda’s historic downtown produces a different claim profile. The city rebuilt substantially after Hurricane Charley in 2004 and again after Ian in 2022, but the historic core retains older structures built to earlier standards. Crews working on them encounter unknown conditions, aging and sometimes hazardous materials, and demolition work in confined spaces — a higher injury exposure than new construction, and one governed by Florida’s construction rule requiring coverage from the first employee. Waterfront hospitality at Fishermen’s Village, by contrast, sits in non-construction classifications where the four-employee threshold applies and seasonal staff count toward it.
