Subcontractor Chains and the Fort Myers Coverage Trap

Florida sets two different workers’ compensation thresholds, and Fort Myers employers routinely operate on both sides of the line. In the construction industry, coverage is required from the very first employee. In non-construction businesses, the requirement generally begins at four employees, counting part-time and seasonal staff. A contractor who also runs an office, or a property company with both maintenance crews and administrative staff, can have both rules applying inside the same business.

The provision that causes the most expensive surprises here is statutory contractor liability. Florida makes a contractor responsible for ensuring that its subcontractors carry workers’ compensation. When a sub does not, that sub’s employees are generally treated as the contractor’s own employees for coverage purposes — meaning an injury to someone you never hired, never paid directly, and never supervised can land on your policy and your experience rating. In a market with subcontractor chains as deep as Fort Myers’, verifying certificates for every tier is not administrative busywork.

The rebuild activity following Hurricane Ian added a wrinkle that persists. A significant number of contractors came into Lee County from out of state to work storm repairs, and out-of-state workers’ compensation policies generally do not satisfy Florida’s requirement for work performed in Florida. General contractors here learned to check not just whether a sub has a policy but whether it is a Florida-compliant one, and subs arriving from elsewhere are often surprised to find their home-state coverage rejected.

Exemptions are the other area where Fort Myers contractors get into trouble. Florida allows corporate officers and LLC members in the construction industry to elect an exemption, but only with at least ten percent ownership, and a corporation is limited to three exemptions. Sole proprietors and partners in construction are treated as employees and cannot exempt out at all. Crews structured to put everyone on an exemption tend to exceed those limits, which leaves uncovered workers on the job and the contractor exposed.
