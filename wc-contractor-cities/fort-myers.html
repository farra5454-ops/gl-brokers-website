Workers' Comp in the Lee County Seat

Fort Myers is our home market. It is the Lee County seat and the hub of the region's construction work, from downtown River District renovations and medical buildings off Colonial Boulevard to the subdivisions going up along Daniels Parkway and the corridors east of I-75. Its larger commercial jobs also tend to run through several layers of subcontractors. On a typical commercial job, a GC hires subs, those subs hire their own crews, and Florida law can make the contractor at the top responsible for the workers' comp of every one of them.

That is why workers' comp in Fort Myers is less about buying a policy and more about getting the details right: the correct class codes, accurate payroll, a valid exemption where one applies, and a certificate on file for every sub you use. Who Needs Workers' Comp in Fort Myers

Florida Statutes § 440.02 sets two different thresholds, and many Fort Myers businesses fall under both.

Construction Contractors: From the First Employee

Roofers, framers, electricians, plumbers, concrete crews, and every other construction trade need coverage as soon as they have one employee. Corporate officers and LLC members count as employees unless they hold a valid state exemption. There is no small-crew allowance.

Non-Construction Employers: At Four Employees

The restaurants and shops of the River District, medical and professional offices, and service businesses along Cleveland Avenue need coverage at four employees. Part-time and seasonal workers count, so a winter staffing increase can trigger the requirement partway through the year.

Mixed Businesses: Both Rules at Once

A contractor with an office, or a property company with maintenance crews and admin staff, applies the construction rule to the field work even if the total headcount is under four. The construction work decides the rule, not the size of the company.

Out-of-State Crews: A Florida Policy Required

Many contractors came to Lee County from out of state after Hurricane Ian. Under § 440.10(1)(g), employers with employees working in Florida need a Florida policy or endorsement that uses Florida class codes and rates, so a home-state policy alone may not be enough.

City Limits or Lee County? Why Your Job Address Matters

A "Fort Myers" mailing address does not always mean the City of Fort Myers. Many properties with a Fort Myers address, including areas such as Gateway and the neighborhoods around the airport, are in unincorporated Lee County. Permits inside city limits go through the City of Fort Myers, unincorporated areas go through Lee County, and nearby cities such as Cape Coral and Fort Myers Beach have their own building departments.

The workers' comp rule is the same at every one of those counters. Florida Statutes § 440.103 requires proof of workers' compensation coverage, or a valid exemption, before a building permit is issued. If your certificate or exemption has lapsed, the permit waits, and so does the job.

Subcontractor Chains: The Fort Myers Coverage Trap

The most expensive surprise in Fort Myers workers' comp is statutory contractor liability. Under Florida Statutes § 440.10, when a subcontractor has no workers' comp, its employees are generally treated as the contractor's employees. An injury to someone you never hired, never paid directly, and never supervised can become your claim and can affect your experience mod.

It also costs you at audit. Each year your carrier compares the payroll you estimated with what you actually paid, and money paid to subs who could not produce a certificate is typically added to your payroll and billed at your rates. For a GC running several subs, that can mean a large additional premium bill after the policy year ends.

The fix is simple and it has to happen every time:

Collect a current workers' comp certificate, or a state exemption, from every sub before they start.

Verify it through the Florida Division of Workers' Compensation proof-of-coverage records, not only the paper certificate.

Track expiration dates. A certificate that lapses mid-job leaves you exposed for every day after it ends.

Keep the certificates together for your audit. Auditors ask for them, and missing ones turn into premium.

Exemptions: What Florida Allows, and What It Doesn't

Florida lets owners of construction companies opt themselves out of coverage, but the rules are narrow. Under § 440.02, no more than three corporate officers or LLC members per company may be exempt, and each must own at least 10% of the business. Under § 440.05, each must also be listed with the Florida Division of Corporations, and an exemption is valid for two years.

Two points catch Fort Myers contractors most often. First, an exemption covers only the owner who holds it. The moment you hire your first employee, you need a policy. Second, § 440.02 counts a sole proprietor in the construction industry as an employee, and the construction exemption is built for corporate officers and LLC members, so a sole proprietor generally cannot exempt out. Crews that try to put every worker on paper as an officer run into the three-exemption limit quickly, which leaves workers uncovered and the business exposed to a stop-work order.
