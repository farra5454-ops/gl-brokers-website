Over-Water Work and the Coverage Gap Cape Coral Crews Miss

Cape Coral was built around a canal network exceeding four hundred miles, and that single design fact creates a workers’ compensation issue found almost nowhere else in Florida at this scale. Crews building seawalls, driving dock pilings, and installing boat lifts spend their working hours over navigable water, frequently operating from barges or floating platforms. That location can pull an injury out of Florida’s state workers’ compensation system and into the federal Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act.

The consequence is a genuine coverage gap. A Florida state workers’ compensation policy is not designed to respond to a Longshore claim, and a marine contractor carrying only state coverage can find an injured employee’s claim falling outside what the policy addresses. Whether Longshore applies depends on the specific nature and location of the work, which is exactly why marine contractors in Cape Coral should have their operations reviewed against that question rather than assuming a contractor policy written for inland work reaches over-water activity.

For the much larger group of land-based crews, the governing rule is Florida’s construction threshold: coverage is required from the first employee. Pool builders, screen enclosure installers, framers, roofers, and concrete crews all sit in construction classifications, and there is no small-crew allowance. A two-person operation in Cape Coral has the same statutory obligation as a fifty-person one, and the city’s building division verifies license and compliance status through the permitting process.

Premium in this market is driven by classification accuracy as much as by payroll. Marine construction, pool work, and roofing carry materially different rates, and a crew that performs several of these functions needs its payroll allocated to the right class codes rather than lumped into one. Misclassification cuts both ways — it can inflate premium unnecessarily, and it can trigger a substantial audit adjustment when the carrier discovers the actual scope of work performed.
