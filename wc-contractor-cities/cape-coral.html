Over-Water Work and the Longshore Coverage Gap

Cape Coral was planned around its canals. The City of Cape Coral describes more than 400 miles of navigable waterways, and that one fact creates a workers' comp issue few Florida cities face at this scale. Crews building seawalls, driving dock pilings, and installing boat lifts spend much of the day over or beside navigable water, often working from a barge or floating platform.

That location matters. According to the U.S. Department of Labor, the federal Longshore Act generally applies when an injury happens on navigable waters or an adjoining area such as a pier or dock, and the worker's job meets the Act's maritime employment test. Florida's own law steps aside when that happens: Florida Statutes § 440.09(2) says Florida benefits are not payable for an employee covered by the Longshore Act.

The result can be a real gap. A marine contractor carrying only a standard Florida policy may find an injured employee's claim outside what that policy was written to cover. The usual fix is a USL&H endorsement on the workers' comp policy. Whether Longshore applies depends on the specific work and where the injury happens. The Act also has exclusions, such as certain workers building or repairing recreational vessels under 65 feet, so Cape Coral marine contractors should have their operations reviewed rather than assuming either answer.

Land-Based Crews: Florida's One-Employee Rule

Most Cape Coral crews work on land, and for them Florida's construction rule governs. Under Florida Statutes § 440.02, construction businesses need coverage from their first employee. Pool builders, screen enclosure installers, framers, roofers, and concrete crews are all in construction classifications, so a two-person crew carries the same obligation as a fifty-person company.

Subcontractor coverage matters too. Under § 440.10, a contractor is generally liable for the workers' comp of a subcontractor's employees when that sub has no coverage of its own. That is why many Cape Coral builders collect a certificate or exemption from every sub before scheduling them on a job.

For permits, § 440.103 requires proof of workers' comp coverage, or a valid exemption, when an employer applies for a building permit. Cape Coral permits are handled by the city's Development Services department. Its Permitting Services Division can confirm the current documents it accepts for your permit type.

Class Codes Drive Cape Coral Premiums

In this market, premium depends on classification accuracy as much as on payroll. Marine construction, pool work, roofing, and framing carry very different rates, and premium is calculated from the payroll in each class code. A crew that does several of these jobs should have its payroll split between the correct codes, backed by records, rather than lumped into one.

Getting it wrong works against you both ways. The wrong code can inflate your premium, and an audit that finds work you didn't report can produce a large additional bill after the policy year ends.
