What Is Commercial Property Insurance?

Commercial property insurance in Florida (sometimes called business property insurance or building and contents coverage) pays to repair or replace the physical things your business owns or is responsible for: the building itself, your equipment and tools, furniture, computers, inventory, signage, and the improvements you've made to a leased space. If a fire guts your Fort Myers shop, a hurricane tears the roof off your Cape Coral warehouse, or thieves clean out your Naples storefront, commercial property insurance is the policy designed to respond.

For Florida business owners, property coverage takes more care than almost anywhere else in the country. Hurricane and windstorm exposure, separate named-storm deductibles, flood exclusions, roof age limits, and a shrinking list of carriers willing to write coastal risks all affect what you can buy and what it costs. The right policy is built around how your building is constructed, where it sits, and how long you could survive being closed.

Commercial property covers damage to your property. Injuries or damage you cause to others are covered by general liability insurance, which is why most Florida businesses carry both, often packaged together in a Business Owner's Policy (BOP).

Is Commercial Property Insurance Required in Florida?

No Florida statute requires most businesses to carry commercial property insurance — but lenders, landlords, and contracts usually do.

When Lenders Require It

  • Any commercial mortgage on your building, usually with the lender named as mortgagee
  • Equipment loans and leases on machinery, truck-mounted equipment, and heavy equipment
  • SBA and bank loans secured by business assets or inventory
  • Flood coverage on buildings in FEMA high-risk flood zones with federally backed loans

When Leases & Contracts Require It

  • Commercial leases requiring tenants to insure their contents and tenant improvements
  • Landlords requiring proof of coverage before you move in or renew
  • Franchise agreements with minimum property and business income limits
  • Condo and commercial association rules for unit owners

What Does Florida Commercial Property Insurance Cover?

Typically Covered

  • Building — the structure you own, including permanently installed fixtures, roof, and systems.
  • Business Personal Property — equipment, tools, furniture, computers, and inventory, whether you own or rent the space.
  • Tenant Improvements — build-outs and upgrades you paid for in a leased space.
  • Covered Perils — fire, lightning, windstorm and hail (subject to your hurricane deductible), theft, vandalism, and sudden water damage from burst pipes.
  • Business Income & Extra Expense (when added) — lost income and continuing expenses while a covered loss keeps you closed.
  • Equipment Breakdown (when added) — sudden mechanical or electrical failure of HVAC, compressors, coolers, and electrical panels.
  • Ordinance or Law (when added) — the extra cost of rebuilding to current Florida Building Code after a major loss.

Not Covered (You'll Need Separate Coverage)

  • Flood, storm surge, and rising water — requires NFIP or private flood insurance
  • Wind on some coastal properties — may require a separate wind-only policy
  • Injuries or damage you cause to others — requires general liability insurance
  • Business vehicles — requires commercial auto insurance
  • Tools and equipment while on job sites or in transit — requires inland marine / contractor's equipment coverage
  • Wear and tear, mold, and gradual leaks or maintenance issues
  • Employee injuries — requires workers' compensation insurance

What Affects the Cost of Commercial Property Insurance in Florida?

Florida commercial property is rated building by building. Two businesses on the same street can pay very different premiums based on construction, roof, and storm protection.

Rating FactorWhy It Matters in Florida
Location & distance to coastCoastal Lee, Collier, and Charlotte County properties carry higher wind and surge exposure than inland properties.
Construction typeMasonry and concrete block buildings typically rate better than wood frame.
Roof age, shape & materialOlder roofs can mean higher rates, actual cash value roof settlements, or a declined application.
Wind mitigation featuresImpact windows, hurricane shutters, roof straps, and secondary water barriers can earn credits.
Year built & code complianceBuildings built to the post-2002 Florida Building Code generally rate better.
OccupancyA restaurant with open cooking rates differently than an office or a contractor's warehouse.
Deductible choicesA higher hurricane deductible lowers premium but raises what you pay after a storm.
Insured value & valuationReplacement cost costs more than actual cash value but pays far more after a loss.

These factors are general Florida market considerations for illustration only and are not a quote. Your actual premium depends on your specific building, location, occupancy, values, loss history, and the carriers available for your risk. Contact us for an exact, no-obligation quote.

Florida Hurricane, Wind & Flood: What Business Owners Should Know

Named-Storm Deductibles

Most Florida commercial property policies apply a separate hurricane or named-storm deductible, usually a percentage of the building's insured value (commonly 2%–5%) instead of a flat dollar amount. On a $1,000,000 building, a 5% deductible means the first $50,000 of a hurricane loss is yours.

Flood Is Always Separate

Storm surge and rising water are excluded from standard property policies. The FEMA National Flood Insurance Program offers up to $500,000 of building and $500,000 of contents coverage for commercial properties, and private flood carriers can write higher limits.

Wind-Only & Citizens Options

When private carriers won't write wind on a coastal property, Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida's state-created insurer under Florida Statutes § 627.351(6), offers commercial property and commercial wind coverage for eligible risks.

Business Income After a Storm

After Hurricane Ian, many Southwest Florida businesses had building coverage but no business income coverage, leaving payroll and rent uncovered for months. Business income and extra expense coverage is one of the most important additions for any Florida business that can't operate without its location.
