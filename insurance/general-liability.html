What Is General Liability Insurance?

General liability insurance in Florida (sometimes called commercial general liability insurance, business liability insurance, or CGL coverage) protects your business from the financial fallout of third-party claims — meaning claims brought by someone outside your company, like a customer, vendor, or member of the public. If a customer slips and falls at your Fort Myers storefront, a client's laptop is damaged during a cleaning job in Naples, or your Tampa marketing agency is accused of copyright infringement in an ad campaign, general liability insurance is the policy designed to respond.

For Florida business owners, general liability insurance is typically the very first commercial insurance policy purchased, and for good reason: Florida's enormous population, heavy tourism traffic, and litigious business climate combine to create real, everyday liability exposure for nearly every type of business, from home-based consultants to large commercial contractors.

Most Florida businesses that carry general liability insurance also carry workers' compensation insurance, since the two policies protect against very different risks — general liability covers claims from customers and the public, while workers' comp covers your own employees.

Is General Liability Insurance Required by Law in Florida?

Florida does not mandate general liability insurance across the board the way it requires workers' compensation — but in practice, most Florida businesses need it anyway.

When Florida Law Requires It

  • Licensed contractors under the Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board
  • Certain trades requiring state or county licensing
  • Businesses operating under specific municipal or county contractor registration rules

When Contracts & Practical Reality Require It

  • Commercial landlords and property managers before you sign a lease
  • General contractors before subcontractors can work a job site
  • Government and municipal contracts throughout Florida
  • Event venues, wedding venues, and hotels before vendors are allowed on-site
  • Client contracts and franchise agreements

What Does Florida General Liability Insurance Cover?

Typically Covered

  • Bodily Injury — medical costs and legal defense if a customer or visitor is hurt at your business.
  • Property Damage — repair or replacement costs if your business damages someone else's property.
  • Personal & Advertising Injury — claims like libel, slander, or copyright issues in your marketing.
  • Legal Defense Costs — attorney fees and court costs, even for claims that are ultimately groundless.
  • Products Liability & Completed Operations — exposure from products sold or work performed, common across many industries.

Not Covered (You'll Need Separate Coverage)

  • Employee injuries — requires Florida workers' compensation insurance
  • Professional mistakes or bad advice — requires professional liability / E&O insurance
  • Damage to your own building or equipment (including hurricane damage) — requires commercial property insurance
  • Business vehicle accidents — requires commercial auto insurance
  • Data breaches and cyberattacks — requires cyber liability insurance

A standard Florida general liability policy covers third-party bodily injury claims (like a customer slipping at your business), property damage you or your business cause to someone else's property, and personal and advertising injury claims such as libel, slander, or copyright infringement in your marketing. Most Florida policies are written with $1,000,000 per-occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate limits as a standard baseline, which satisfies most landlord and contract requirements.

General liability insurance does not cover employee injuries (that's what Florida workers' compensation insurance is for), professional mistakes or bad advice (that requires professional liability or errors and omissions insurance), or damage to your own business property from a hurricane or fire (that requires commercial property insurance). Many Florida business owners combine general liability with these other policies through a Business Owner's Policy (BOP) for broader, often more affordable, protection.
